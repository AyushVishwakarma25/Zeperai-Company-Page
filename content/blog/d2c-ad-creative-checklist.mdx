Most D2C brands don't have a targeting problem. They have a creative problem — and they find out the expensive way, after the budget is already spent.

Before your next ad goes live, run it through these seven checks.

## 1. Does the hook land in the first 2 seconds?

If someone can't tell what the ad is about — or why they should care — before they've finished their first scroll, the rest of the creative doesn't matter. The hook is doing 80% of the work.

## 2. Is the offer clear without reading the caption?

The visual and headline should carry the offer on their own. Captions get cut off, muted, or skipped entirely on mobile feeds.

## 3. Does it show the product being used, not just the product?

Static product shots on a white background rarely outperform creative that shows the product solving a real, specific problem in context.

## 4. Is there a single, obvious next step?

One clear call-to-action beats three vague ones. If the viewer has to think about what to do next, you've already lost them.

## 5. Have you tested more than one hook?

Creative testing isn't optional at this point — it's the fastest lever you have. Two or three hook variants on the same core creative usually surfaces a clear winner within days.

## 6. Does it match how people actually talk?

Ad copy that sounds like a press release gets scrolled past. Copy that sounds like a friend's recommendation gets read.

## 7. Would you stop scrolling for it?

The most honest test is also the simplest. If you wouldn't stop for it in your own feed, don't expect anyone else to.

---

Getting even three of these seven right consistently is usually enough to move the needle on ROAS. Getting all seven right, repeatably, is what a creative system is for.
