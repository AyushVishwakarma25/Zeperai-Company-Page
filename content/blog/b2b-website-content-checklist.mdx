A prospective client rarely calls a B2B firm or service business cold. They visit the website first — and decide, often in under a minute, whether this business looks like it can be trusted with something important.

Here's where most B2B sites lose that decision before the conversation even starts.

## The homepage talks about the company, not the client's problem

Visitors don't care how many years you've been in business until they know you understand the specific problem they came to solve. Lead with the problem, not the origin story.

## There's no proof at the top of the page

Case studies buried three clicks deep don't do their job. The strongest proof point you have should be visible without scrolling.

## The site looks like a template

Generic layouts signal a generic business. For firms competing on trust and craft — interior designers, consultancies, agencies — a templated site actively works against the pitch.

## There's no clear next step

"Contact us" is not a call-to-action, it's a dead end. A specific next step — "Book a 20-minute discovery call" — converts because it removes ambiguity about what happens next.

## The content assumes the reader already trusts you

Content that explains *how* you work, not just *that* you work, builds trust with someone who has never met you. That's the entire job of a B2B website: closing the trust gap before the first call.

---

None of this requires a bigger budget — it requires the site to be built around the client's decision, not the company's org chart.
